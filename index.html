Grafting Overview: Why & How

💡 Why Graft Plants?

  • Clonal Genetic Accuracy: Seeds pass wild/unpredictable traits. Grafting produces 100% identical clone fruit.
  • Speed to Harvest: Reduces fruiting time from 7–10 years (seedling) down to 2–3 years.
  • Disease & Soil Resistance: Uses hardy rootstocks immune to nematodes, root rot, and soil salinity.
  • Dwarfing & Space Efficiency: Controls canopy size for easy hand harvesting in dense orchards.

🛠️ General How-To Steps

  1. Select Rootstock & Scion: Choose compatible, healthy plant tissue of matching stem diameter.
  2. Align Cambium Layers: Make clean diagonal/wedge cuts so the vascular inner bark layers touch precisely.
  3. Seal & Bind: Wrap tightly with Parafilm tape to lock in humidity and prevent air/water leakage.
  4. Aftercare & Pruning: Prune all shoots growing below the graft union so nutrients nourish the scion.

Mango Grafting

Method: Side-Veneer / Cleft
Why: Ensures high yield & early fruit maturity (2-3 yrs vs 8+ yrs seed).

Sweet Orange Grafting

Method: T-Budding / Chip Budding
Why: Imparts root-rot resistance and sweet fruit consistency on Trifoliate stock.

Lemon & Lime Grafting

Method: T-Budding / Cleft
Why: Protects roots from Phytophthora fungal infections in wet soils.

Avocado Grafting

Method: Cleft / Side-Tongue
Why: Clones commercial Hass scions onto disease-tolerant root bases.

Apple Grafting

Method: Whip & Tongue
Why: Controls tree height on dwarfing M9/M26 rootstocks during dormancy.

Coffee Grafting

Method: Hypocotyl Cleft
Why: Fuses high-flavor Arabica scions with nematode-resistant Robusta roots.

Guava Grafting

Method: Patch Budding / Cleft
Why: Overcomes wilt disease and propagates seedless cultivars true-to-type.

Passion Fruit Grafting

Method: Splice / Cleft
Why: Joins purple sweet scions onto yellow Fusarium-wilt-resistant root bases.

Papaya Grafting

Method: Side Veneer / Cleft
Why: Guarantees 100% hermaphrodite fruit-bearing trees without non-bearing males.

Macadamia Grafting

Method: Bark Graft / Whip & Tongue
Why: Propagates high-oil commercial nut cultivars onto robust taproot seedling bases.

Cleft Grafting

Difficulty: Intermediate
Stem Size: Medium to Large (2.5–8cm)
Ideal for top-working older rootstocks or branches with larger stem diameters.

T-Budding

Difficulty: Beginner
Stem Size: Pencil Thin (6–12mm)
Popular for citrus and stone fruits during active spring/summer sap flow.

Whip & Tongue

Difficulty: Advanced
Stem Size: Pencil Thin (6–12mm)
Provides maximum cambium contact for young branches of identical thickness.

Side Veneer

Difficulty: Intermediate
Stem Size: Pencil to Medium (8–15mm)
Excellent for evergreen tropical trees like mangoes and avocados in warm weather.

Bark Grafting

Difficulty: Intermediate
Stem Size: Thick Trunk (5–15cm+)
Top-working method for top canopy renewal on mature stumps when bark is slipping.

Chip Budding

Difficulty: Intermediate
Stem Size: Pencil Thin (6–12mm)
Versatile budding method used anytime during active or dormant seasons when bark won't slip.

Regional Grafting Windows

  • Late Winter / Early Spring: Deciduous trees (Apples, Pears, Peaches). Best for Whip & Tongue and Cleft grafting while dormant.
  • Active Growth / Rainy Season: Tropical/Evergreen trees (Citrus, Mangoes, Avocados, Coffee). High humidity and active bark slipping favor T-budding and Side-Veneer grafts.
  • Late Summer / Autumn: Chip budding and late T-budding before growth halts for temperate species.
