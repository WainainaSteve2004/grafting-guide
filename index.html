Beginner Friendly Dormant Season

Cleft Grafting

Ideal for top-working older trees or converting rootstocks to new cultivars during dormant winter/early spring.

Intermediate Active Growth

T-Budding (Shield Budding)

Widely used for citrus and stone fruits when bark is actively slipping during summer months.

Intermediate Dormant Season

Whip & Tongue Grafting

Provides maximum cambium contact and structural interlocking for thin scions and rootstocks of equal thickness.

Advanced Active Growth

Side Veneer Grafting

Perfect for difficult-to-graft tropicals like Mango and Avocado, allowing the rootstock foliage to remain attached during callus development.

Intermediate Spring Bark Slipping

Bark Grafting

Designed for grafting small scion twigs onto large, mature trunk or branch stumps when sap is flowing in spring.

Versatile Dormant or Active

Chip Budding

Can be performed even when bark is not slipping, making it an excellent all-season alternative to T-budding.

Primary Fruit & Tree Propagation Guide

Click any tree species to explore reasons for grafting, recommended rootstocks, and specific step-by-step grafting procedures.

Mango (Mangifera indica)

Side Veneer & Cleft Grafting

Grafting eliminates seed polyembryony variations, reduces fruiting time from 8 years to 2-3 years, and ensures true-to-type sweetness.

Sweet Orange (Citrus sinensis)

T-Budding & Chip Budding

Grafting provides immunity to Phytophthora root rot, controls canopy size, and vastly accelerates fruit production.

Lemon & Lime (Citrus spp.)

T-Budding & Cleft Grafting

Prevents soil-borne fungal pathogens and ensures high acid juice quality and early year-round yields.

Avocado (Persea americana)

Cleft & Side Veneer

Seedlings produce unpredictable fruit; grafting commercial cultivars (Hass, Fuerte) guarantees fruit yield and oil content.

Apple (Malus domestica)

Whip & Tongue / Chip Budding

Apples do not grow true from seed. Grafting onto dwarfing rootstocks (M9, MM106) controls size and boosts fruiting speed.

Coffee (Coffea arabica / canephora)

Hypocotyl / Cleft Grafting

Combines high-quality Arabica scions with nematode-resistant Robusta rootstocks to survive infested plantation soils.

Grafting onto peach rootstocks provides drought tolerance and prevents bacterial canker in cold spring zones.

Cleft & T-Budding

Serves both as a prized aromatic fruit and as an essential dwarfing rootstock for commercial pear propagation.

Universal Grafting Rules Across Any Tree Species

Whether grafting apples, mangos, figs, or rare timber trees, every successful graft relies on four fundamental botanical principles.

1

Botanical Affinity

Grafting only succeeds between closely related plants. Best results occur within the same species (intra-species) or genus.

2

Cambium Alignment

The vascular cambium—the thin green growth ring under bark—of both scion and rootstock MUST physically touch to form vascular bridges.

3

Moisture Sealing

Exposed cuts dry out quickly, killing callus cells. Wrap tightly with Parafilm or budding tape to seal in 100% humidity.

4

Scion Dormancy

Scion wood must be dormant (buds not yet swelling) while the rootstock is either dormant or actively pushing sap.
