Fruits & Tree Guide

Detailed guides on all grafted fruit plants: Why we graft them and general step-by-step how-to procedures.

Mango Grafting

Primary Method: Side-Veneer / Cleft
Why Graft: Accelerates bearing (2–3 yrs vs 8+ yrs seed), guarantees true-to-type fruit sweetness, and manages canopy size.

Sweet Orange Grafting

Primary Method: T-Budding / Chip Budding
Why Graft: Imparts soil fungus resistance (Phytophthora) using Trifoliate rootstocks and prevents seed polyembryony variations.

Lemon & Lime Grafting

Primary Method: T-Budding / Cleft
Why Graft: Prevents root rot in wet soils, ensures uniform citrus acidity, and doubles early fruit production density.

Avocado Grafting

Primary Method: Cleft / Side-Tongue
Why Graft: Clones commercial Hass/Fuerte varieties onto root-rot tolerant bases, skipping decade-long juvenile delays.

Apple Grafting

Primary Method: Whip & Tongue / Cleft
Why Graft: Controls tree vigor with dwarfing rootstocks (M9/M26), yields uniform orchard spacing, and improves cold hardiness.

Coffee Grafting

Primary Method: Hypocotyl Cleft Grafting
Why Graft: Fuses high-flavor Arabica scions with nematode-resistant Robusta roots to protect plantations from root pests.

Grafting Methods Masterclass

Explore the six primary propagation techniques, run the Method Finder, or review technical specifications.

Cleft Grafting

Difficulty: Intermediate
Stem Size: Medium to Large (2.5–8cm)
Ideal for top-working older rootstocks or branches with larger stem diameters.

T-Budding

Difficulty: Beginner
Stem Size: Pencil Thin (6–12mm)
Popular for citrus and stone fruits during active spring/summer sap flow.

Whip & Tongue

Difficulty: Advanced
Stem Size: Pencil Thin (6–12mm)
Provides maximum cambium contact for young branches of identical thickness.

Side Veneer

Difficulty: Intermediate
Stem Size: Pencil to Medium (8–15mm)
Excellent for evergreen tropical trees like mangoes and avocados in warm weather.

Bark Grafting

Difficulty: Intermediate
Stem Size: Thick Trunk (5–15cm+)
Top-working method for top canopy renewal on mature stumps when bark is slipping.

Chip Budding

Difficulty: Intermediate
Stem Size: Pencil Thin (6–12mm)
Versatile budding method used anytime during active or dormant seasons when bark won't slip.

Regional Grafting Windows

  • Late Winter / Early Spring: Deciduous trees (Apples, Pears, Peaches). Best for Whip & Tongue and Cleft grafting while dormant.
  • Active Growth / Rainy Season: Tropical/Evergreen trees (Citrus, Mangoes, Avocados, Coffee). High humidity and active bark slipping favor T-budding and Side-Veneer grafts.
  • Late Summer / Autumn: Chip budding and late T-budding before growth halts for temperate species.
